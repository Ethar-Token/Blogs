OUR EDITORIAL STANDARDS

Editorial Policy

Our editorial policy explains how we approach the stories we publish, the standards we aim to uphold and our responsibility to readers.

Last updated: November 20, 2026

Our commitment: We Said It aims to publish engaging, thoughtful and responsible editorial content. We value accuracy, transparency, originality, fairness and respect for the people and subjects discussed in our articles.

1. About Our Editorial Policy

This Editorial Policy explains the principles and standards that guide the creation, review and publication of content on We Said It.

We Said It is an independent editorial blog covering subjects such as human behaviour, relationships, dating, gossip, internet culture, unusual experiences, social trends and opinions.

Our aim is to publish content that is interesting, relevant and worth reading while treating information and the people discussed in our articles responsibly.

This policy applies to the editorial content published on our website and the processes involved in preparing that content.

2. Editorial Independence

Our editorial decisions are guided by the relevance of a subject, its value to readers, the quality of available information and our editorial judgement.

We aim to maintain independence in deciding what to publish, how to approach a topic and what conclusions or perspectives an article presents.

Advertisers, sponsors, commercial partners and other outside parties do not automatically receive the right to influence our editorial decisions.

Commercial relationships should not determine the factual conclusions, opinions or editorial positions expressed in our independent content.

3. Accuracy and Fact-Checking

We aim to make our articles accurate and avoid presenting unsupported claims as established facts. The level of verification applied depends on the nature and significance of the subject.

Where an article contains factual claims, we aim to check relevant details before publication. This may include:

  • Checking names, dates, figures and other specific details.
  • Consulting original documents and reliable sources where available.
  • Comparing information across sources when appropriate.
  • Checking quotations and their surrounding context.
  • Distinguishing confirmed information from claims that remain unverified.
  • Reviewing whether an article gives readers enough context to understand the subject.

We do not intend to publish fabricated facts, invented quotations or deliberately misleading information.

When reliable verification is not possible, we may qualify a statement, explain the uncertainty or decide not to include it.

4. Sources and Attribution

We recognise the importance of giving credit to original sources and allowing readers to understand where information comes from.

Depending on the article, our sources may include:

  • Official statements and public records.
  • Research papers, studies and published reports.
  • Recognised publications and established media sources.
  • Expert commentary and relevant professional information.
  • Publicly available information from organisations and institutions.
  • First-hand accounts where their origin and limitations are made clear.

Where we rely on another publication, study, document or source for material information, we aim to provide appropriate attribution or a link where practical.

We do not intentionally misrepresent the identity, authority or statements of a source.

Information found online is not automatically treated as accurate simply because it has been published or widely shared.

5. Opinion, Commentary and Editorial Content

We Said It publishes content that may include commentary, interpretation, observations and opinion alongside factual information.

We believe readers should be able to distinguish between a verifiable factual statement and an interpretation or personal viewpoint.

Opinions may be direct, critical, humorous or unconventional. However, presenting an opinion does not give us a reason to knowingly misrepresent facts or make unsupported accusations about identifiable people.

Where a topic involves disputed information, we aim to make that distinction clear and provide appropriate context.

6. Headlines, Titles and Descriptions

Headlines and article titles help readers understand what a story is about and decide whether to read it. We aim to make them clear, relevant and engaging.

We may use curiosity, strong wording, humour or provocative framing where appropriate to the subject. However, headlines should not deliberately create an impression that the article itself does not support.

We aim to avoid:

  • Headlines that materially misrepresent an article.
  • False claims designed only to attract clicks.
  • Descriptions that remove important context.
  • Exaggerations that turn speculation into apparent fact.

Search descriptions, social media captions and promotional material should also represent the underlying article fairly.

7. Fairness, Privacy and Respect

We aim to discuss people, groups and sensitive subjects with appropriate care and respect.

When an article concerns an identifiable person or organisation, we aim to avoid unnecessary personal exposure, misleading representations and unsupported allegations.

We consider the potential impact of publishing personal details, particularly where the subject involves children, vulnerable individuals, private relationships or sensitive circumstances.

Where serious allegations are discussed, we aim to seek relevant responses or include available context where reasonably practicable.

We do not believe that public curiosity alone justifies exposing someone's private information.

8. Images, Videos and Other Media

Images and other media can help readers understand an article, but they should not create a false impression about the subject.

We aim to use media that is relevant to the article and that we have the right or appropriate permission to use.

Depending on the material, images may be:

  • Original photographs or graphics.
  • Licensed or otherwise permitted third-party images.
  • Illustrative or representative images.
  • Media embedded from external sources where permitted.

Illustrative images should not be presented in a way that falsely suggests they depict an actual person, event or situation.

We aim to respect copyright, attribution requirements and the rights of people appearing in visual media.

9. Use of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence tools may be used as part of the editorial and content preparation process where appropriate.

Such tools may assist with tasks including:

  • Brainstorming article ideas and possible angles.
  • Organising research and outlining content.
  • Grammar, spelling and language improvements.
  • Summarising material for further human review.
  • Formatting, editing and other production assistance.

AI-generated output is not automatically treated as accurate or authoritative. Factual claims, quotations, references and other important details should be checked before publication.

AI tools do not replace editorial responsibility. We remain responsible for the content we choose to publish.

We do not intend to knowingly present fabricated AI-generated events, quotations, sources or evidence as genuine.

Where synthetic or substantially altered media could mislead readers about what actually happened, we aim to identify its illustrative or generated nature.

10. Originality and Copyright

We value original editorial work and respect the intellectual property rights of other creators.

We do not intentionally copy another publication's work and present it as our own.

When using information, quotations, images or other material created by others, we aim to provide appropriate attribution and respect applicable copyright requirements.

Our articles may discuss, reference or link to material published elsewhere. Such references do not imply ownership of the original material.

11. Advertising and Sponsored Content

We Said It may display advertising or participate in commercial partnerships as part of operating the website.

Advertising and independent editorial content should remain distinguishable.

Where content is sponsored, paid for or produced as part of a commercial arrangement, we aim to identify that relationship clearly.

Advertisers and commercial partners should not be able to purchase favourable conclusions or control independent editorial decisions simply by advertising on the website.

The presence of an advertisement does not mean that We Said It independently recommends or endorses the advertised product or service.

12. Updates and Corrections

We recognise that mistakes can happen and that information may change after an article is published.

We aim to review reported errors and make appropriate corrections when necessary.

Corrections

When we identify a material factual error, we aim to correct it as promptly as reasonably possible.

Where a correction significantly changes the meaning or understanding of an article, we may include a note explaining what was corrected.

Clarifications

If wording is unclear or lacks important context, we may revise the relevant section to make the meaning more accurate or understandable.

Updates

Articles may be updated to include new information, reflect developments or replace outdated details. An update does not necessarily mean the original article contained an error.

Minor spelling, punctuation, formatting and broken-link fixes may be made without a separate correction note where they do not change meaning.

13. Removal Requests and Editorial Review

We may receive requests to amend, clarify or remove published material.

Each request should be considered on its individual circumstances.

We may consider:

  • Whether the information is factually inaccurate.
  • Whether the article creates a misleading impression.
  • Whether personal information has been exposed unnecessarily.
  • Whether there are legitimate privacy or safety concerns.
  • Whether a legal obligation applies.
  • Whether an update or correction would resolve the issue.

A disagreement with an article or an unfavourable opinion does not automatically mean the material must be removed.

We aim to assess requests fairly and respond appropriately based on the information available.

14. Reader Feedback and Complaints

We welcome constructive feedback about our published content, including concerns about factual accuracy, sourcing, context or fairness.

If you believe an article contains an error, please contact us with:

  • The title or URL of the article.
  • The specific passage or detail you are concerned about.
  • An explanation of why you believe it is inaccurate or misleading.
  • Supporting information or reliable sources, where available.

Providing clear details helps us review a concern more effectively.

We may request further information where necessary to understand the issue.

15. What We Do Not Intend to Do

Our editorial standards are intended to prevent practices that undermine reader trust.

We do not knowingly intend to:

  • Fabricate facts, quotations, sources or events.
  • Present unverified allegations as established facts.
  • Deliberately mislead readers through headlines or descriptions.
  • Copy another creator's work and claim it as our own.
  • Present illustrative media as genuine evidence.
  • Conceal material commercial relationships in sponsored content.
  • Allow advertising relationships to dictate independent editorial conclusions.
  • Publish private or identifying information without appropriate consideration.

16. Changes to This Editorial Policy

We may update this Editorial Policy as our website, editorial practices and publishing standards develop.

Changes may also be made to reflect new technologies, lessons learned from our publishing processes or applicable requirements.

When this policy is updated, we will revise the "Last updated" date at the top of this page.

We encourage readers to review this page periodically.
