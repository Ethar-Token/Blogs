Things People Do in Secret: 15 Weird Habits That Are Insane

From talking to yourself to rehearsing arguments in the shower, here are 15 secret things people do when no one's watching, and why they're normal.

We Said It · November 19, 2026
Advertisement

Everyone has a secret life. Not the dramatic, spy-movie kind, but the small, strange, oddly specific things we only do when the door is shut and nobody is watching.

Some of these habits sound unhinged when you say them out loud. The truth is that most of them are far more common than you think. Psychologists, sleep researchers, and behavioral scientists have studied many of them, and the findings are surprisingly reassuring.

Here are 15 things people do in secret (yes, you probably do a few of them), plus what's really going on behind each one.

1. They Talk to Themselves Out Loud

Giving yourself a pep talk, narrating your to-do list, or arguing with an imaginary coworker all count. It feels embarrassing, but research on self-talk suggests that speaking to yourself, especially in the second or third person ("Okay, you've got this"), can improve focus and help you manage stress.

Verdict: Not insane. Basically a productivity hack.

2. They Win Entire Arguments in the Shower

The shower is where people deliver flawless speeches to bosses, exes, and relatives who aren't there. This is a form of mental rehearsal. Your brain is processing emotion and practicing how you'd like a conversation to go.

Verdict: Normal, and you always win these debates.

3. They Smell Things They Probably Shouldn't

A partner's shirt, a loved one's old sweater, a new book, even their own clothes. Scent is closely tied to memory and emotion, which is why a familiar smell can feel instantly comforting.

Verdict: Weird, but wired into how our brains work.

4. They Eat Dinner Standing Over the Sink

No plate, no table, just a fork and the glow of the fridge. Sometimes it's a random assortment of snacks that somehow counts as a meal, a habit the internet has playfully nicknamed "girl dinner."

Verdict: Chaotic, relatable, and almost universal.

5. They Sing and Dance Like Nobody's Watching (Because Nobody Is)

Full-volume singing, choreographed living-room routines, and hairbrush microphones. Music and movement reliably lift mood, and doing it in private removes the fear of being judged.

Verdict: Not insane. It's free therapy.

6. They Check Up on Someone's Social Media

An ex, an old friend, a rival, or a person they met once. Social media makes it easy to keep tabs on others without anyone knowing, and curiosity and social comparison are deeply human.

Verdict: Extremely common, though it can feel worse than it's worth.

7. They Rewatch the Same Comfort Show Forever

They've seen it ten times and can quote it, yet they put it on again. Rewatching familiar media is comforting because there are no surprises, which makes it a low-stress way to unwind.

Verdict: A cozy habit, not a lack of imagination.

8. They Talk to Their Pets Like Tiny People

Full conversations, complete with opinions on the pet's life choices, and sometimes a voice for the pet's replies. Bonding with animals often involves treating them as part of the family.

Verdict: Adorable. Pets don't judge.

9. They Make Faces and Practice Conversations in the Mirror

Rehearsing a job interview, testing a smile, or practicing a difficult confession. Preparing for social situations helps many people feel more confident when it counts.

Verdict: Smart preparation.

10. They Stay Up Late on Purpose

It's 1 a.m., they're exhausted, and they're scrolling anyway. This even has a name: revenge bedtime procrastination, where people give up sleep to reclaim personal time after a busy, controlled day.

Verdict: Understandable, but your sleep schedule won't thank you.

11. They Fall Into Elaborate Daydreams

Imagined careers, alternate lives, perfect vacations, or movie-worthy scenarios. Everyday daydreaming is normal and linked to creativity and planning. If daydreaming starts to interfere with daily life or feels out of control, it's worth talking to a mental health professional.

Verdict: Usually healthy, and the source of many great ideas.

12. They Google Their Symptoms at Midnight

A headache becomes a rare disease within three searches. The urge to self-diagnose online is so common that researchers have a term for the anxiety it can create: cyberchondria. A doctor is always a better source than a search bar.

Verdict: Very human, but not recommended.

13. They Hide Purchases or Stash Money

Some keep a private savings account, hide shopping bags, or quietly delete a receipt. People do this for many reasons, from financial security to avoiding an argument. In relationships, hidden money can erode trust, so open communication usually works out better.

Verdict: Common, but risky for trust.

14. They Cry Alone to Sad Songs

A song, a movie scene, or a memory, and suddenly the tears come. Many people find that crying alone feels like a release, and sad music can help people process emotions safely.

Verdict: Healthy. Emotions need an exit.

15. They Pick, Bite, or Fidget Without Realizing

Nails, skin, lips, hair twirling, and pen chewing are often unconscious habits that show up during stress, boredom, or concentration. Occasional habits are normal. If one causes pain, injury, or distress, a doctor or therapist can help.

Verdict: Common, and manageable with support.

Why Do We Do Weird Things When Nobody's Watching?

In public, we manage how others see us. In private, that filter turns off. The things we do alone tend to be what comfort, stress relief, curiosity, and creativity look like when there's no audience.

The key takeaway is that being strange in private is a normal part of being human. Most of the behaviors above are harmless, often helpful, and shared by far more people than anyone admits.

When a Secret Habit Becomes a Problem

Most private quirks are harmless. It may be time to seek support if a habit:

  • Causes pain, injury, or health problems
  • Costs you money you can't afford to lose
  • Damages your relationships or involves ongoing deception
  • Feels compulsive or impossible to stop
  • Leaves you feeling distressed or ashamed

A licensed therapist or doctor can help without judgment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are some weird things people do when they're alone?

Common examples include talking to themselves, singing and dancing, rehearsing conversations, eating odd meals, rewatching favorite shows, and staying up late scrolling.

Is it normal to talk to yourself?

Yes. Self-talk is very common, and research suggests it can help with concentration, motivation, and emotional regulation.

Why do people have secret habits?

People keep habits private to avoid judgment or embarrassment, or because the behavior is deeply personal, comforting, or stress-relieving.

When should I worry about a secret habit?

If a habit causes harm, feels out of control, costs significant money, or affects your relationships or mental health, consider talking to a professional.

Final Thoughts

The things people do in secret may look insane from the outside, but they're usually just people being people: coping, creating, rehearsing, and recharging. So the next time you catch yourself dancing with a spoon at midnight, don't be embarrassed. You're in very good company.
